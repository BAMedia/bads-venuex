Where to eat

No Yelp ratings (Yelp blocks automated loads). Michelin pages loaded this run. Rotated picks.

Zuni Café, Market St, SF (1658 Market St) · Californian · in the Michelin Guide. Michelin calls it a San Francisco institution with a laid-back California vibe, locally sourced food and bold artwork-covered walls. A few blocks from the opera house for Sunday's Manon matinee. (415) 552-2522. https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/california/san-francisco/restaurant/zuni-cafe

Nari, Japantown, SF (Hotel Kabuki, 1625 Post St) · Thai · Michelin One Star. A sleek retreat with a dramatic setting and accomplished cooking from Chef Pim Techamuanvivit. (415) 868-6274. https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/california/san-francisco/restaurant/nari

Sorrel, Pacific Heights, SF (3228 Sacramento St) · Contemporary · Michelin One Star. Chef Alexander Hong, trained at Quince, has a gift for pasta; a rooftop garden supplies herbs, edible flowers and vegetables. (415) 525-3765. https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/california/san-francisco/restaurant/sorrel

Anchor Oyster Bar, Castro, SF (579 Castro St) · seafood (carried). Tiny old-fashioned counter spot known for crab cakes, chowder, raw oysters and cioppino. Right by Saturday's Castro Halloween, so expect crowds. Hours unconfirmed. https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/california/san-francisco/restaurant/anchor-oyster-bar

Where to stay

Per SF Travel, Union Square has most major hotels and the best transit links, a short walk to SFMOMA and Yerba Buena Gardens for Saturday; Hayes Valley and Civic Center put you by the opera and Zuni; Mission Bay is closest to Chase Center for both Warriors games. Skip Marina/Van Ness-area hotels Sunday morning if you need to drive out before noon (half-marathon closures). https://www.sftravel.com/neighborhoods
